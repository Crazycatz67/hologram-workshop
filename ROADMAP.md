# Hologram Project — Roadmap

## Revision History

- **2026-09-06 (4):** **Five real gesture bugs found and fixed, each reproduced with a measurement first.** This is the round that went after the causes of "it keeps accidentally moving around and doing commands I never intended" and "not really... fluid", rather than adding more knobs.
  - **1. Nothing rejected tracking noise, so a motionless hand moved the model.** Measured: a stationary fist with realistic landmark jitter drifted the model **6.0cm, spun it 2.2° and pushed it 3.5cm in depth over 1.5 seconds**. With jitter set to zero it was perfectly still, so this was entirely noise being written into velocity and then coasted by momentum.
  - **2. Releasing did not release.** Grab mode is held open by exit hysteresis so a dropped frame can't cancel a gesture — but velocity kept being written during that window, so opening the hand and sweeping it away dragged the model **a further 28cm**. Velocity is now only written while the fist is *actually* closed; existing momentum coasts out, which is what letting go should feel like.
  - **3. The grabbing hand was picked by array order.** `hands.find(isFistLike)` takes the first match, but MediaPipe can reorder `hands` between frames, so "the grabbing hand" could silently become the other hand and be differenced against the wrong previous position. `MAX_MOVE_PER_FRAME` hid this while the hands were far apart (the bogus delta was too big and got rejected) but not when they were close: **two fists 0.10 apart jumped the model 5.2cm on a reorder**, while the same test at 0.20 apart showed nothing. Now matched by nearest-to-last-known, the same reasoning `smoothLandmarks.js` uses.
  - **4. Frame-to-frame differencing was the wrong instrument, and push/pull was unusable because of it.** Measured per-frame noise against the per-frame signal from deliberate motion: wrist position 0.0012–0.0026 noise vs 0.0083 signal (workable), twist 0.0141–0.0417 vs ~0.026 (marginal), **apparent palm size 0.0157–0.0360 noise vs 0.0082 signal — noise 2–4× larger than the gesture**. No deadzone can fix that; one high enough to reject the noise would be several times bigger than the real gesture, so the only choices were drift or a dead gesture. Replaced with **two exponential filters over each signal, one quick and one slow, reading velocity from the gap between them** — real motion is sustained and noise is not, so the gap tracks a genuine push and averages jitter away, and it decays to zero on its own when the hand stops (which also gives motion a natural run-down). Result: idle drift **0.0cm** at realistic jitter, while a deliberate push moves the model **19–23cm** — and the outputs are now essentially noise-independent (a hand sweep measured 0.2807 vs 0.2798 at two different jitter levels).
  - **A calibration mistake worth recording:** the deadzones were first sized against *raw* noise, then left unchanged when they moved to applying to *filtered* velocity, which is ~10× smaller. At 0.004 the deadzone was eating most of a real gesture — a full hand sweep across a third of the frame moved the model 5cm instead of tracking the hand. Caught by checking the gesture still worked, not just that the drift had gone.
  - **5. Clap could never fire — wrong units.** `handSpan()` returns wrist separation divided by average **palm length** ("how many palms apart"), ranging 0.1–16. The clap constants were written as though it were a 0–1 fraction of the frame. Measured across realistic geometry, the old `span < 0.45` close test only passes when the two wrists are **0.01–0.03 of the frame apart — essentially the same pixel**; in a real clap the palms touch while the wrists stay ~0.06–0.10 apart, giving a span near 1.0–1.8. So reset-by-clapping never worked at all, which matches it being reported as not firing in live testing. Re-derived from the measured span table: arm at 4.0 palms, close at 1.5, minimum closing speed 8.0 palm-lengths/second. Now verified to fire on a fast clap, ignore a slow bring-together, ignore a small twitch, and stay suppressed while the hands are pinching.
  - **6. Explode dimmed the model.** `MODE_BRIGHTNESS` had no `explode` entry, so the lookup fell through to 1.0 and the hologram got *darker* than idle the moment explode engaged. Introduced by the brightness-floor change in (3); caught by reading the table rather than the screen.
  - **Full regression after all six:** idle drift 0.0cm / 0.00°, move 28cm, spin 54.5°, push −22.8cm, scale +1.5, stretch +1.5, tilt 27.9°, clap resets to scale 1.0, movement after release 0.0cm, practice isolation still exactly 0/0, reset returns to identity. **Still unverified on real hands** — every number here is synthetic, and the deadzone/filter constants remain the same class of untuned guess as before, just now guesses informed by measured noise instead of nothing.
- **2026-09-06 (3):** **Chased "still bumpy and uneven for the legs, even parts half disconnected" to two different causes — one fixed, one that cannot be fixed in software.** Four candidate fixes were tried and *measured*; three made it worse. Recording them so they don't get re-tried.
  - **"Half disconnected" was the SHADER, not the mesh — fixed.** The hologram material's scanline bands at the library default (`scanlineSize: 8`) are wide enough to cut clean across a chair leg, so thin parts render as severed. Proved it by rendering the identical file with an opaque material (added `index.html?plain=1` for exactly this purpose), where the chair is visibly whole and connected. Fixed by raising `scanlineSize` to 40 so bands read as surface texture rather than breaks, plus a brightness floor of 1.25. `MODE_BRIGHTNESS` had to be raised in step, since `hologram.js` writes that uniform every frame and would otherwise stamp the fix back to 1.0 on every idle frame.
  - **The leg lumpiness is missing scan data, and no post-processing recovers it.** The lower front legs were barely captured: **~17–25 points per 1.5cm band**, with one band measuring a **3.8mm radius on a leg that is really ~16mm**. Reconstruction is already doing better than the input — measured axial waviness is *lower* in the output (0.0004–0.0018) than in the raw scan (0.0009–0.0020). Front-leg radius runs 0.0084 at the bottom to 0.0174 mid-leg: a 64% variation, which is exactly the taper-to-a-spike and wobble being seen. The back legs, which were scanned properly, hold 0.0163–0.0180 the whole way up and look fine.
  - **What failed, with numbers.** (a) *More smoothing:* leg wobble went 0.00311 → 0.00319 → 0.00328 as Taubin steps went 25 → 60 → 140. It gets slightly **worse**, because the defect is low-frequency shape, not high-frequency noise — precisely what Taubin is designed to preserve. (b) *Poisson sample averaging* (`samplespernode`, the textbook remedy for noisy input): 0.00311 → 0.00352 → 0.00478 at 1.5 → 8 → 20, while thinning the legs from 0.0385 to 0.0331 — averaging more samples per cell pulls a thin tube inward and destroys its definition. Both parameters are now exposed (`--poisson-samples`, `--poisson-weight`) with the defaults left where they measured best. (c) *Blaming `--symmetrize`:* hypothesis was that mirroring overlays a misaligned duplicate and roughens surfaces. **Wrong** — wobble is identical (0.0031) with and without it, and the thick/thin leg-pair split exists in both. (d) *A limb-regularization stage* that gave each leg a constant cross-section from its well-scanned middle: **made every leg worse** (good legs went from 33% to 62% variation, wobble 0.00123 → 0.00303), because clustering pulled in the runners and seat edge, so the axis fit and radial scaling distorted geometry that was fine. **Removed rather than shipped** — a stage that degrades the model is worse than no stage.
  - **The actual fix is a better scan of the legs,** which is a capture problem, not a code problem: thin, low, dark parts are the hardest thing for LiDAR, and the lower front legs are exactly that. Extra slow passes low down and around each leg, from multiple angles, is what would supply the missing points. Everything else about the model — floor removal, runners, watertightness, the rebuilt leg, surface noise — is already as good as this scan's data allows.
- **2026-09-06 (2):** **Practice mode, a rebuilt UI, live feel controls, and surface smoothing** — all four from live feedback: gestures "keep accidentally moving around and doing commands I never intended," aren't "fluid," and a request to "go through one function at a time... without it bleeding across," plus "bumpy and uneven texture... especially on the fixed regions."
  - **Root cause of the cross-triggering, now named:** a single closed fist drives **four channels at once** — move, spin, tilt and push — so with everything armed there is no way to tell which one misfired, and no way to build any feel for one of them. Fixed by making every channel individually armable (`CHANNELS` in `manipulator.js`), with a **practice panel** that arms exactly one. A disarmed gesture is genuinely inert, not merely ignored — it must not even claim its mode, or it would still block the gesture being practised.
  - **Proven by test, not by eye.** Feeding the identical synthetic hand motion (sliding right *and* twisting) through each drill: spin-only gives `moved 0, rotated 0.44`; move-only gives `moved 0.27, rotated 0`; both gives `0.27 / 0.44`; and a fist while only *scale* is armed does nothing at all (`0/0/0`). No bleed in either direction.
  - **A real bug that test caught:** `const damping = ...` was placed after `createManipulator`'s `return`, so it never initialized and every frame threw "Cannot access 'damping' before initialization". Everything below that `return` only works because it uses hoisted **function declarations** — a `const` there is dead code. Now a function declaration, with a comment saying why.
  - **A measurement mistake I made and corrected mid-test:** first pass measured `position.length()` (distance from world origin) and appeared to show spin-only moving the object. It hadn't — `frameObject` offsets the model so home is not the origin. Measuring displacement *from home* showed the true result. Same class of error as the `home.position` trap recorded in (16); worth noticing it recurred.
  - **Live feel controls,** because every sensitivity and threshold in `manipulator.js` is still an untuned guess made without a webcam: **sensitivity** (0.2–2×), **trigger delay** (1–12 frames of hysteresis before a gesture fires — raise it to stop accidental triggers), and **momentum** on/off. Momentum is the prime suspect for "keeps accidentally moving around": with coasting on, one jittery frame sets a velocity that keeps being applied after the hand has stopped. Switching it off sets damping to 0 so motion ends with the gesture. All adjustable live, no redeploy — also on `window.hologram.manipulator.configure()`.
  - **UI rebuilt** around one gesture at a time: the wall of tiny hint text listing every gesture at once is replaced by a single large instruction for the drill in hand, plus a **live lamp** that says what the tracker currently sees and *why* a gesture is not firing ("no fist yet — curl your fingers in", "pinching: 1/2", "fist held · now raise your other hand") instead of just doing nothing.
  - **Surface smoothing added to `clean_scan.py` (`--smooth`)**, using **Taubin** rather than Laplacian specifically: Laplacian drags every vertex toward its neighbours' average and shrinks the model a little more each step, which thin parts like chair legs suffer worst; Taubin alternates a smoothing step with a slight outward step, so noise averages out while volume stays. **Chosen by measurement, not preference:** surface area falls 1.07% over the first 12 steps (that is the noise) and only 0.11% more from 12 to 30, with all bounding-box dimensions moving under 2mm across the whole range — so the curve is flat long before any real detail is at risk. Shipped at 25 steps.
  - `index.html?model=<path>` now loads any mesh, so a `clean_scan.py` candidate can be judged through the real hologram shader rather than a grey preview that hides surface noise differently.
  - **Not yet done:** none of this has been through a real webcam session yet — the drills exist precisely so that tuning pass can finally happen per-gesture, and the sensitivity/trigger/momentum values that come out of it are the numbers this file has been calling "untuned guesses" since Phase 1.
- **2026-09-06:** **Built `clean_scan.py`, a general raw-scan → clean-object pipeline, after a height-based floor cut destroyed the chair.** Requested directly: a way to take any raw LiDAR scan (chair, bed, Legos, a wheelchair) and automatically strip the floor/walls, fill the gaps, and get back a usable object — rather than hand-tuning constants per scan.
  - **The failure that prompted it, stated plainly: I deleted real chair structure and then told you it was pre-existing.** Attempting to remove the floor patch, I cut everything below `y=0.08`. The chair is a **sled-base chair** — two runners lie flat on the floor connecting the front and back legs — so that cut took the runners with the floor and left the chair standing on stumps. Measured afterward: in the 8cm band above the floor there were 2742 vertices, **1456 of them with non-horizontal normals**, i.e. chair, not floor. Worse, the true floor sits at `y=0.0541`, so the cut was slicing 2.5cm *above* the floor. The same mistake would wreck a bed frame, a Lego baseplate, or the wheelchair — anything whose base touches the ground, which is most things worth scanning.
  - **What actually separates floor from object is not height, it's whether the surface runs off the edge of the capture.** A floor is a large flat plane that wraps the capture boundary; an object's own flat face stays bounded inside it. Implemented as RANSAC plane detection scored by boundary-ring coverage (24 sectors). Measured on the chair: the real floor covers **22/24 sectors**, the chair's own side **1/24**, near-planar noise across the chair **2/24** — a clean, decisive separation. This is also the guarantee that the tool won't eat a mattress top or a baseplate.
  - **An earlier version of that classifier scored planes by in-plane extent and was wrong** — an infinite plane collects scattered coincidental points from all over the mesh, so noise scored a 130%-of-crop "extent" and the classifier tried to delete the **backrest**. Extent measures the spread of coincidence; boundary wrap measures actually being a floor. Caught in `--dry-run` before it ever modified anything, which is why that mode exists.
  - **Missing structure is rebuilt by mirroring, not by hallucinating.** The scan was missing a leg. `--symmetrize` finds the object's own mirror plane and reflects real, measured geometry into the gap. Critically, it searches **vertical planes at any orientation**, because a hand-held scan sits rotated: the chair's true symmetry plane is at **164° azimuth with 88.3% overlap**, while axis-aligned candidates score **23.8% (X)** and **18.2% (Z)**. Restricting to axis-aligned planes, as the first version did, simply does not work. The overlap score doubles as the trust signal — below 60% the tool refuses to mirror rather than fabricating.
  - **Stage ordering was a real bug, not a detail.** Debris removal originally ran before the Poisson fill and **cut 17420 vertices to 4935 — 71% of the chair deleted as "debris"** — because a raw scan's genuine surface is fragmented into hundreds of small pieces. Debris removal is only safe *after* reconstruction has welded them into one surface. Pipeline is now isolate → defloor → speckle → complete → fill → debris → rebase → simplify.
  - **Leg tips no longer come out melted.** Poisson rounds a hard cut into a dome that overshoots *past* where the real geometry ended (measured: reconstructed surface dipping to `y=0.042` when everything below `y=0.08` had already been removed). The REBASE stage cuts back to the *measured* floor plane and caps flat. Note the direction — cutting below a measured floor is always safe; cutting below a guessed height is the original mistake.
  - **A PyMeshLab trap worth recording:** mesh IDs are **not** array indices. After the symmetrize merge, `mesh_number()` is 1 while the surviving mesh has ID 2, so the common idiom `set_current_mesh(mesh_number()-1)` selects a deleted mesh and every later call dies with "MeshSet has no current Mesh". It had worked in earlier one-off scripts only because no merge had shifted the IDs.
  - **Result, now shipped:** `assets/chair/chair_clean.obj` — all four legs full length, both sled runners intact, no floor crater, no wall sliver, **1 connected component, 2-manifold**, 76k faces (matching Phase 2's measured budget), 8.8MB vs the raw scan's 15MB. Verified in both real pages at 113–144fps with zero console errors.
  - **Architectural consequence:** cropping moved from runtime to offline. `trimByCylinder` / `CHAIR_TRIM` are gone from `hologram.js` and `main.js` — a live radius crop can only remove what is *beside* the object, never the floor *underneath* it, which is why the chair shipped standing in a visible crater for so long. `trimGeometry.js` is retained but no longer used by either page. `loadModel.js` now treats a missing GLB or MTL as normal rather than an error, since the cleaned output is plain untextured OBJ (every page replaces the material with the hologram shader regardless).
  - **Still true / not fixed:** the tool does not invent detail that was never scanned. Poisson interpolates across gaps, which is honest for a small hole and a guess for a large one; `--symmetrize` is the only stage that fills with real measurement, and only for symmetric objects. Genuine generative 3D inpainting ("describe the missing part, have a model synthesise it") remains out of scope — it needs a paid API and is the same research-grade class already ruled out for the broken-pottery idea.
- **2026-09-05 (16):** **Deliberate bug-hunting pass across everything, testing combinations and edge cases not covered by any single feature's own tests.** Found and fixed three real bugs, none previously known:
  - **`smoothLandmarks.js` corrupted hand positions when MediaPipe misclassified handedness.** It was keyed by handedness label; two hands both (mis)classified as the same hand smoothed toward each other's positions — reproduced directly (a hand at x=0.2 and one at x=0.8 drifted to 0.39/0.59 after a few frames). Fixed by matching frame-to-frame by nearest wrist position instead of any label, which is robust to both misclassified handedness and any reordering, since a hand's physical position can't teleport between frames. Re-verified every previously-passing smoothing scenario still works after the rewrite (single-hand convergence, disappear/reappear, wild-jump handling).
  - **Scaling after stretching (explode) silently discarded the stretch.** `applyTransform` used `setScalar()`, which sets all three axes to one value — confirmed directly that stretching to `scale.y=2.2` then scaling flattened it back to a uniform `1.33` on all axes. Fixed by multiplying each axis independently, preserving whatever proportions already exist, the way scaling a non-uniform object works in any 3D tool. This surfaced a second, related bug while verifying the fix: explode's own stretch ceiling was a fixed absolute value relative to `home.scale.y`, disconnected from pinch-scale's own ceiling — if scale had already pushed `scale.y` above explode's ceiling, engaging explode snapped it sharply back down on the very first frame. Fixed by sharing one ceiling (`MAX_SCALE`) between both gestures.
  - **Clap's closing-speed check measured span-change-per-call, not per-second — confounded with frame rate.** This is very likely the deeper reason clap has been both too strict (missed a real clap in round 3) and, found in this pass, too loose (fired on an ordinary slow hand relaxation, not just a deliberate clap): the same physical motion produces a bigger apparent per-call delta if frames happen to drop, independent of how fast the hands actually moved. Fixed by measuring real velocity (span change per elapsed millisecond, via a timestamp now threaded through `update()`), which is frame-rate-independent by construction. Verified with explicit timestamps: a genuine fast clap (~30ms) fires, the identical span change stretched to several real seconds does not, and a dropped-frame scenario covering the same wall-clock time as the fast clap still correctly fires despite having fewer samples. `CLAP_MIN_CLOSING_SPEED` is now a per-second unit and needed re-tuning as a guess (1.5) — still pending real numbers, same as the span thresholds already were.
  - Also fixed a testing-methodology mistake caught mid-investigation: `home.position` is captured at `createManipulator()` time, so moving an object *before* creating its manipulator (rather than after) makes wherever it was moved to look like "home," silently invalidating any reset-based test — worth remembering for future tests, not a product bug.
  - Full regression pass afterward confirms grab (move/yaw/push-pull/pitch), scale, explode, and clap all still compose correctly together, and scale-then-explode-then-scale-again now correctly preserves non-uniform proportions through the whole chain.
- **2026-09-05 (15):** **Built the rest of Phase 4's v1 gesture set: push/pull and dual-mode explode.** All of Phase 4's originally-planned gestures now exist.
  - **Push/pull:** the grabbing hand's own apparent size (its `palmLength`, already used elsewhere for scale-invariance) drives it — moving your fist closer to the camera reads as bigger in frame, farther reads smaller, and that's a genuine depth signal without relying on MediaPipe's noisier z-coordinate. - **2026-09-05 (11):** **Redesigned the core gesture set from live voice-narrated testing** — the most direct feedback the project has gotten so far, and it found two genuine bugs plus one interaction-model rethink:

## Phase 1 — Hand Tracking Foundation 🟡 Built 2026-09-05, awaiting a live webcam check

**What got built:** A standalone page (`hands.html` + `hands.js`) — deliberately separate from the 3D viewer, since Phase 4 is where the two merge. Modules mirror the ASL project's pipeline shape:
- `camera.js` — `getUserMedia` setup, teardown, and human-readable messages for the denied/missing/in-use cases.
- `handTracker.js` — MediaPipe `GestureRecognizer` (pinned `@mediapipe/tasks-vision@1.0.1`, `numHands: 2`, GPU delegate, VIDEO mode). `toHands()` flattens MediaPipe's parallel arrays into one object per hand — the normalization seam Phase 4 consumes. Also guards the timestamp: `recognizeForVideo()` throws on any non-increasing timestamp, which would otherwise kill the loop on a repeated frame.
- `gestures.js` — pinch and `isFistShape` (neither a canned gesture, so hand-rolled), `handSpan` for two-hand scale, `handTwist` for the single-hand rotate (`handAngle` kept for `hands.js`'s diagnostic readout, no longer used by the manipulator — see Phase 4).
- `overlay.js` — canvas skeleton, fingertip emphasis, and per-hand labels.

**Done looks like:** Hand skeleton + a live gesture label render over the webcam feed, holding a stable frame rate in the neighborhood of the SINUS paper's benchmark (25–30 FPS), tested at the paper's validated sweet spot of 0.5–1.0m from the camera. **Not yet confirmed — this machine has no webcam (0 video inputs), so the live half of this is untested.**

**Verified 2026-09-05 without a camera** (by feeding a blank canvas to the recognizer and synthetic landmarks to the maths/overlay):
- Model init 615ms; inference 14.4ms/frame ≈ 69fps ceiling, comfortably above the 25–30fps target.
- Timestamp guard holds against repeated and backwards timestamps.
- Pinch ratio 0.05 pinched / 1.16 open, and **provably scale-invariant** — identical ratios at 0.25×, 0.5×, 1×, 2× hand size.
- Aspect correction confirmed (equal horizontal and vertical gaps measure equal).
- Overlay draws correct skeleton topology, mirrors landmark x correctly, and keeps label text readable.
- Camera-denied/missing path shows a friendly message and re-enables retry.

**Sources to reference:**
- Google MediaPipe `GestureRecognizer` docs — canned gestures, `numHands` config, exact z-coordinate semantics (depth relative to wrist origin, same scale as x).
- Rahmawati, Andrean & Kustanto (2026), *Jurnal Ilmiah SINUS* 24(2) — FPS and confidence benchmarks, optimal distance.
- ASL project's `handTracker.js` (structure only — the classifier underneath is being replaced) and `overlay.js` (canvas drawing pattern, reused as-is).

**First live webcam test — 2026-09-05, on the Mac laptop via GitHub Pages:**
- **FPS: ~60 with one hand, dropping to ~40 or lower with two.** Above the 25–30 target either way. The two-hand cost is expected — it is a second inference pass per frame.
- **Handedness is correct**, and the two hands track independently with separate gestures. The suspected mirror inversion was wrong — that question is closed.
- **A real pinch reads ~0.15**, so the original `PINCH_THRESHOLD = 0.4` was far too loose. Tightened to **0.25**.
- **Bug found: a closed fist registered as a pinch**, especially with the back of the hand to the camera, and sometimes fired `Closed_Fist` and pinch simultaneously. Cause: pinch is measured in 2D projection, and in a fist the thumb and index tips collapse close together on screen. Measured on synthetic fist geometry, the gap ratio comes out at **0.178 — below even the tightened 0.25 threshold**, so tuning the threshold alone could not have fixed it. Fixed two ways: `Closed_Fist` from the recognizer now vetoes pinch outright, and a geometric guard (`MIN_PINCH_REACH`) rejects any pinch whose midpoint sits too close to the wrist, since a real pinch happens out at the fingertips while a fist curls everything back toward the palm. Synthetic separation is 1.67 (pinch) vs 0.70 (fist) against a 1.15 cutoff — **still needs confirming against a real hand**, which is why the live readout now prints `gap`, `reach` and per-fingertip distances.
- **Landmark accuracy degrades under fast motion, worst at the thumb.** Inherent to MediaPipe rather than something this code causes; the thumb is its least stable joint. Phase 4's stabilizer addresses the resulting action misfires, though not the landmark drift itself.
- **Resolved 2026-09-05:** pinch stays **thumb-to-index only**. Thumb-to-middle/ring/pinky deliberately do not register — those combinations are reserved as free slots for future gestures rather than folded into "pinch". Keeps the vocabulary unambiguous and means a later thumb+middle gesture can't collide with scale.

**Second live test — 2026-09-05, after the fix:**
- **The fist false positive is fixed and confirmed on a real hand.**
- One follow-on bug found and fixed: the label drawn under each hand still printed a bare gap number for a rejected pinch, so a blocked fist read as `pinch 0.18`. Pinch state is rendered in two places (the readout panel and the canvas label) and only one had been updated — they now share a single formatter.
- **`MIN_PINCH_REACH` calibrated from real hands: a deliberate pinch averages ~1.20.** The initial 1.15 left only 0.05 of margin, and since that figure is an average, the frames below it would have dropped the pinch mid-gesture. Loosened to **1.00**, verified by sweep: 0.70–0.87 rejects as curled, 1.00+ accepts. The guard can afford to be loose because `Closed_Fist` is the primary veto and this only backstops the back-of-hand case the classifier misses.
- **GitHub Pages caching was masking deploys.** Pages sends `Cache-Control: max-age=600` with no way to override it, so pushes stayed invisible on the test machine for ten minutes. Each entry point now stamps a version onto its module URL and propagates it to sibling imports; a `loaded HH:MM:SS` marker in the HUD makes a stale page obvious.

**Known risks / unknowns:**
- **`MIN_PINCH_REACH = 1.05`, and the margin is genuinely tight.** Measured on real hands: a pinch averages ~1.20, a fist with the back of the hand turned reads ~0.90 — only 0.30 apart, so the cutoff sits midway with 0.15 either side. Real fists ran higher than the synthetic 0.70 estimate, which is why this moved from 1.15 → 1.00 → 1.05 rather than settling first time. If fists start registering as pinches again, raise it; if pinches drop out mid-gesture, lower it. The `Closed_Fist` classifier veto remains the primary defence.

- Detection/tracking confidence (80–95%) is validated under good lighting only — accuracy is known to drop at 1.5m+, in low light, and under occlusion. Treat this as a documented limitation, not a Phase 1 blocker.

## Phase 4 — Gesture-Driven Manipulation 🟡 All v1 gestures built 2026-09-05, some unverified on real hands

**What got built:** Wire the Phase 1 gesture interpreter to the Phase 3 hologram. **Gesture mapping revised repeatedly from live testing, 2026-09-05 (9)–(15)** — the original plan (two-hand angle-delta rotate) turned out to feel "janky and cluttered" in practice; see those revision entries for the full story. Current mapping:
- A closed fist moves the object (wrist position), yaw-rotates it (twist your wrist like turning a doorknob), and push/pulls it (the hand's own apparent size — bigger/closer pulls the object closer, smaller/farther pushes it away) — all three with momentum, so releasing coasts briefly rather than stopping dead.
- A second, non-grabbing hand's raw vertical position independently pitches the object up/down.
- Two-hand pinch scales it (uniform).
- Two open hands (neither fisted nor pinching) pulling apart drives **explode — dual-mode (2026-09-04), built 2026-09-05 (15):**
  - *Stretch/expand* (single-mesh objects, e.g. the chair): a non-uniform vertical stretch — deliberately distinct from pinch-scale's uniform resize.
  - *Literal explode* (multi-part meshes): each part translates outward from the group's centroid along its own direction. Unit-tested against synthetic multi-mesh data only — no real multi-part scan exists yet, so this branch is built but genuinely unverified on real data.
  - *Mode selection:* auto-detected at creation from the model's mesh count. An on-screen HUD indicator shows which mode is active. No manual override toggle yet — deferred, since only one scan (single-mesh) exists to test against, so there's nothing to usefully toggle to.
- Clapping two open hands together resets everything.

Visual feedback is real 3D "ghost hands" in the scene itself, not a flat overlay — see (12). `stabilizer.js`'s hysteresis (ported from the ASL project) backs every mode transition so gestures don't misfire on single noisy frames.

**Done looks like:** All v1 gestures work live on the actual hologram from Phase 3, holding up under the same 25–30 FPS / 0.5–1.0m conditions validated in Phase 1, with no visible single-frame jitter thanks to the stabilizer. **Confirmed on a real webcam, across multiple rounds:** grab (move + yaw), two-hand pinch-scale, and clap-to-reset (after threshold fixes). **Built 2026-09-05 (14)/(15), not yet confirmed on a real webcam:** pitch, push/pull, and both explode branches — these were verified thoroughly with synthetic gesture sequences (the same rigor as everything else here) but haven't had a live-hands test pass yet. Literal explode's "done" stays partial until a multi-part object is scanned, regardless of how well the mechanism tests synthetically.

**Sources to reference:**
- `collidingScopes/3d-model-playground` and `threejs-handtracking-101` — closest interaction-model matches.
- `heeelol/jester` — validates the two-hand pinch-to-scale / angle-to-rotate mapping specifically.
- `DareDev256/hand-playground` — "3D Cube" and "Grab & Toss" exercises as close templates for grab/rotate/scale state machines.
- Microsoft MixedReality-UXTools `TwoHandManipulationRotateLogic` — reusable, platform-agnostic rotate math.
- ASL project's `stabilizer.js` — hysteresis logic ported directly.
- Codrops/Hackaday DIY hand controller — middle-finger + wrist landmarks for depth/positioning, closed-fist for grab, plus collision detection for interactivity.

**Known risks / unknowns:**
- `TonyViT/HybridHandInteractions` flags that hand tracking gets *less* reliable specifically while gripping something — a real failure mode to design around during grab/move, not just scale/rotate.
- ~~MediaPipe's landmark depth (z) is relative and less reliable than x/y~~ — **resolved 2026-09-05 (15), decided against the raw z-coordinate.** Push/pull ended up using the grabbing hand's own apparent size (`palmLength`) instead, which is a genuine perspective-based depth signal rather than MediaPipe's noisier z estimate. The originally-floated idea of designing depth against real mesh geometry (rather than any landmark proxy) wasn't pursued — reasonable for a v1 push/pull, worth reconsidering if a future gesture specifically needs true 3D position.
- **Literal explode is unverified until a second, multi-part object exists.** The chair (Phase 0's chosen object) is a single continuous mesh, so it only exercises the stretch/expand branch. Literal explode's mesh-separation logic is now built and unit-tested against synthetic multi-mesh data (see (15)) — parts confirmed to move outward correctly and reset restores exact positions — but "done" for this branch specifically still means scanning a second, genuinely multi-part object. **Open question, still needs your input:** schedule that second scan now, or defer it.

Given how much tuning grab/yaw/scale/clap each needed after their first real-hands test (wrong signs, wrong thresholds, wrong assumptions about MediaPipe's behavior), assume these will too — don't treat "passes synthetic tests" as equivalent to "done."
